Cowboy Space Corp. calls itself "a future launch operator and satellite constellation licensee,"
and its comment in FCC docket 13-115 explains why reliable access to launch spectrum is
"fundamental" to its plans. The company is developing Stampede, a system of up to 20,000
non-geostationary satellites meant to provide compute from in-orbit data centers. Rather than
rely on third-party launch providers, it is building in-house launch capabilities
dedicated to Stampede and intends to use FCC-licensed spectrum for telemetry, tracking, and
command of its rockets.

The comment, filed September 14, 2026 and signed by Chief Operating Officer and Chief Legal
Officer Joseph Yaffe, asks the Commission to open a rulemaking on launch spectrum. "An
insufficient supply of spectrum will hamstring our nation’s space innovators," it warns. Docket
13-115 holds 219 filings from 81 distinct parties, including 125 comments, with SpaceX,
Relativity Space, and Lockheed Martin among the filers.

> "First, the Stampede system architecture relies on a unique satellite design whereby the operational payloads are integrated into the upper stage of each rocket, thereby maximizing the usable mass to orbit."
> — Joseph Yaffe, Chief Operating Officer and Chief Legal Officer, Cowboy Space Corp., FCC docket 13-115
