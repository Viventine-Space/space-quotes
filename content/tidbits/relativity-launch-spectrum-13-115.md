In reply comments filed September 25 in FCC docket 13-115, Relativity Space says flight test and space launch telemetry are, in nearly every respect the Aerospace and Flight Test Radio Coordinating Council (AFTRCC) identifies as relevant to spectrum priority, "the same type of operation." Both carry real-time safety data, both are intolerant of significant interruption, and both involve high-value hardware. Relativity notes that AFTRCC uses those traits to justify primary status for its incumbent users. Relativity says the same conclusion "could be extended to the commercial space launch community."

One material difference is duration and geographic extent: a launch occupies spectrum "for minutes, from a fixed site or sites," along a trajectory that can be deterministically characterized. Relativity posits that this profile "could make space launch easier to accommodate than additional users requiring sustained spectrum access under a co-primary allocation." The company asks the Commission to consider a co-primary allocation for commercial launch. Orbit Sentinel counts 219 filings in the docket from 81 distinct parties, including 125 comments.

> "Holding commercial space launch operations as secondary users in these bands may therefore be a root cause of capacity concerns raised by the flight test community."
> — Maggie McNeece, Director, Government Affairs, and Kiersten Fortson, Senior Counsel, Launch Operations, in the reply comments of Relativity Space, Inc., FCC ET Docket 13-115
